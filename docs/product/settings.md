# Settings

> Role: **Current**

Settings are organized by the user's decision, not by implementation module.

## Navigation

- **General**: Quick Entry (shortcut, Selected Text, screen and Chrome source), Notifications, Startup (login and Dock), then Troubleshooting (App Logs and Local Records).
- **Appearance**: Color Theme (Follow System, Light, or Dark), Input Background (Cat, Shark, or Off), then Finish Animation.
- **AI Services**: one personal model form, with service and billing details for connection tests.
- **Templates**: editable built-ins and local custom writing templates.
- **Apple Notes**: direct access to save destination, daily grouping and review settings. No app-list step.
- **Writing Preferences**: Automatic Instant Insight, its shared prompt, and collapsed saved background text when the AI capability is present.
- **Quick Guide**: a repeatable version of onboarding.
- **About**: version, update controls, download link, and contact link.

Labels use one to four familiar words. Ordinary descriptions stay within 20 words; privacy, billing, deletion, and essential steps use short sentences without omitting consequences. App names, keyboard shortcuts, and user text keep their original form. AI prompts and saved note section headings retain their existing wording.

The sidebar groups configuration separately from help. Every page has a title above a scrollable, grouped form. Routine pages omit subtitles that repeat their controls; Templates keeps the useful `@` insertion instruction, and action detail pages can retain their action summary. Ordinary help explains a choice rather than repeating its label or stating that settings are saved. AI sharing, billing, permissions, local recording and deletion consequences remain next to the relevant controls or confirmation. Long usage instructions are expandable; errors remain visible. The window opens at 820 × 640 points, supports resizing down to 760 × 560, and limits form width on larger windows. Controls and surfaces follow the selected light, dark, or system appearance.

Appearance applies immediately to the settings window, quick record panel, native editor, and other Jotway windows. Follow System remains the default and tracks the Mac's appearance; Light and Dark override it for Jotway. The existing `themeMode` preference preserves the choice across launches and keeps previously saved selections. Compact theme cards show the name and selection; explanations remain in hover and accessibility help. There is no saved/immediate-application footer. Input Background stores its independent `editorBackgroundStyle` choice locally and changes only the editor decoration immediately. Cat is the default when no recognized value is saved; Shark shows one shark with no seabed, and Off removes the decoration. Switching stops the previous background and preserves the draft and native editor. Finish Animation appears once below Input Background, with its native Drift Away / Fly Away menu and Reduce Motion information in hover and accessibility help. It uses the original app-level `submissionEffect` binding, `wind` / `ufo` encoding and Drift Away default; presentation changes neither runtime animation nor Completion Notifications.

General groups controls in this order: Quick Entry contains the keyboard shortcut, its conflict/recommendation feedback, Selected Text, Use Pointer's Screen and Chrome Source; Notifications contains Completion Notifications; Startup contains Open at Login then Show Dock Icon with its issue message; Troubleshooting contains the initially collapsed App Logs controls followed by Local Records. Each has its own description, export and deletion controls. App Logs excludes draft text and access keys; Local Records includes unsent drafts. App Logs keeps its existing open-folder, export and clear actions without adding logging options. Instruction editors keep restore-default on the left and cancel/save on the right; changes still require an explicit save.

Completion Notifications is a single switch for all actions and defaults to off. The `completionNotificationsEnabled` preference persists across launches and is checked when a result arrives, including tasks already running when the setting changes. Turning it off suppresses completion popups without replaying those successes at the next panel opening. Execution, failure recovery, and the recent Codex task list remain available.

There is no App Suggestions page, intent-service key, connection test, model readiness or billing card. Local application matching needs no credential. Legacy Jev credential files and unrelated preferences are neither read nor removed.

Local Records in General → Troubleshooting defaults to a capture switch, a short disclosure that includes unsent drafts, an Export Records button, and collapsed Manage Records. No outer disclosure hides these controls. The management disclosure contains retention days (unchanged 1–3650 range and 90-day default), space, counts, pending writes and Clear Records. Re-entering General resets only the disclosure state. Healthy integrity is silent; missing/unknown integrity, unavailable statistics, busy state, operation results and Show Exported Files stay outside the fold. Export cancellation restores controls. Stop-and-delete and clear retain separate confirmations; cancellation preserves capture and records. App Logs and Local Records retain separate storage; clearing either leaves the other untouched.

Your Phrases has no settings entry or runtime effect. Existing `intentRules` data remains unchanged but is not loaded, edited or considered effective action configuration. Historical operation records retain their rule sources and snapshots.

AI Services temporarily hides the current text service card and Jotway AI activation and quota controls. Saved configurations and credentials remain intact. A stored code still takes priority for every text request, including connection tests; invalid, disabled, depleted or unreadable codes never fall back to personal credentials. Account refresh and quota state retain their credential-scoped behavior. See [AI capability](../plugins/ai.md#jotway-ai-activation).

The personal model form stays directly visible, without the Personal Model heading or an outer disclosure. A provider picker offers DeepSeek, Kimi, OpenAI, Claude and Custom. Presets supply the official address, protocol and a Get API Key link; existing configurations keep their values. API Base URL and protocol live in Advanced Settings, which opens for Custom. Changing protocol or address clears the entered key and requires re-entry; same-address model edits may retain the saved key. There is one masked key and one model, shared by all text features.

Field labels sit above full-width controls. Credentials, model selection and the save/test area have separate spacing and dividers. Get API Key sits beside the key label; Load/Refresh Models sits beside the model label, with a lightweight manual/list switch below the field. Save Changes is the primary bottom action, Test Generation is secondary, and Remove Key stays separate on the left. Unsaved edits explain why testing is unavailable. Errors and test results use icon-and-text notices next to their actions; request destinations and billing disclosures remain visible. The form scrolls with the settings page rather than clipping long names or feedback.

Load Models explicitly reads the personal service's directory, with its destination disclosed beside the action. It works before saving or choosing a model, uses only a matching saved personal key or the key just entered, and sends no text. Loading can be cancelled. The picker preserves the chosen ID, including IDs absent from the list; manual entry remains available. Empty, partial and failed directories explain the manual fallback. Discovery success does not claim generation support. Editing cancels pending discovery; changes to address, protocol or key clear the old list. Lists stay in memory only, and opening or saving the form starts no requests.

Save, Remove Key and Test Generation remain explicit. A stored Jotway code leaves the personal form editable but inactive and changes the button to Test Jotway AI. A notice and target label identify the actual service/model and billing scope. Tests use fixed sample text and deep streaming, and are disabled until edits are saved; edits, configuration/key changes or leaving the view cancel old results. Directory and generation results are separate. Notes grouping links to this page instead of duplicating credential forms. There are no fallback, separate fast/deep, history-search or embedding selectors.

Automatic Instant Insight is off by default. The existing `automaticDraftThinkingPromptsEnabled` Boolean preserves saved true and false values across restart. When enabled, a pause of about one second sends the current draft to AI using the same prompt, answer rules and limits as manual requests. Loading, empty results and failures stay closed. Turning it off cancels automatic work while keeping manual requests available.

One Instant Insight prompt editor controls both entry points. Save applies the edited behavior; Cancel restores the saved text; Restore Default changes the editor until saved. Blank saves select the bundled default. The independent `instantInsightPrompt.v1` preference persists locally, accepts up to 16,000 UTF-8 bytes, and is sent with each Instant Insight request. It does not change Notes, background instructions, model selection or the automatic switch. Invalid saved values remain stored and block both entry points until repaired; automatic work stays silent. Failed saves preserve the previous effective preference and the edited text. Changing the effective prompt cancels old work and clears its result without generating another answer. See [Instant Insight](../../openspec/specs/draft-thinking-prompts/spec.md).

About Your Writing is collapsed by default, with a visible note that its text is saved on this Mac but is not used by current writing tools. Expanding it preserves access to saved text and the existing Save, Cancel and Reset to Default controls. Its short reference-text placeholder appears only when empty and never becomes the saved value. Collapsing and expanding keeps unsaved edits. Both instruction editors keep Enter/newline and explicit-save guidance in hover and accessibility help, without a permanent footer.

The background value remains in `plugin.ai.manualConfiguration`. Current Instant Insight, Notes title, grouping and review requests do not read it; Settings' Check Connection uses fixed sample text and does not read it either. The retained `AppState.testAIConnection` callback in `Plugins.swift` can compose it into a legacy connection test, but has no current UI caller. This retained callback does not make the background a shared preference for current features.

Jotway currently ships an English-only interface and does not expose a language setting or follow the macOS interface language. App-owned display copy, action labels, dynamic status, and safe errors are resolved from semantic keys in the English package resource. Stable IDs and machine state never store translated text. This resource boundary is the extension point for adding another language later without changing routing, persistence, or action configuration. User-authored text, destination names, and installed application names remain unchanged.

## Usage guide

Existing action-specific settings requests can still open the Codex detail page; Back returns to Apple Notes. Set Up Codex opens a one-time installer in Terminal after the user has installed and signed into Codex CLI. The installer copies the bundled helper into the user's Application Scripts folder; it leaves a different existing helper untouched. Check Setup refreshes its availability. Subsequent tasks run in the background without opening Terminal or selecting a project. See [Codex](../integrations/codex.md).

Quick Guide is a scrollable daily reference as well as a repeatable onboarding page. Its first section shows three steps for opening, entering text, checking the target and confirming, with direct actions to open the quick record panel or configure Apple Notes. The page shows the actual configured shortcut and conflict state, preserves shortcut editing and trial behavior, and links directly to Apple Notes settings. Detailed typing shortcuts, app and AI behavior, draft recovery and privacy use expandable sections. The app and AI section explains automatic note titles, Instant Insight and how to open a review. Manage Local Records opens General and scrolls to its record controls in Troubleshooting. Privacy guidance distinguishes local app matching from text sent to writing services.

The guide covers templates, Apple Notes, local application opening, remaining explicit actions, keyboard behavior, plain-text input limits, independent Notes titles and grouping, in-memory drafts and failure recovery. Content belongs to target applications; local operation records can retain unsubmitted text. Dismissal is not success. Opening or leaving the guide does not replace, submit or clear a draft.

## Action settings

Templates keeps its full syntax reference in a collapsed Template Syntax section. Validation errors remain beside the edited fields.

The independent Templates page shows the two built-in templates first, initially named “记录想法” and “梳理问题”. Each template displays its name above its multiline body. Built-ins enter editing in their existing row and return there after Save or Cancel, keeping their stable identity and fixed order; they cannot be deleted. Custom templates follow creation order, with edit and delete controls below their content, and keep their new/edit form below the list. One pending edit disables all Add, Edit and Delete controls until it is saved or cancelled. Each form places Name above the single-line field, then Body above the full-width multiline editor, with labels, fields and Save/Cancel aligned left. A failed save keeps the entered text and shows the reason in that form; Cancel leaves the saved version untouched. Inline editing focuses the name field and retains Command+S and Esc. Detailed editing and protection rules are in [Input templates](../../openspec/specs/input-templates/spec.md). Apple Notes configuration is available directly from the sidebar.

Names are trimmed, single-line, nonempty and unique ignoring case across built-in and custom templates. Bodies must expand to non-whitespace text. Supported markers are `${n}`, `${n:default}` and at most one `${0}`; positive numbers are unique, have no leading zero and are visited numerically. Escape a dollar with `\$`, a backslash with `\\`, or a default-value closing brace with `\}`. `$1` stays literal. Unsupported syntax shows its UTF-16 position and preserves the editor and previous saved definition.

Template definitions use the versioned `inputTemplates.v1` local UserDefaults value. Custom definitions retain stable UUIDs and list order; optional `builtInOverrides` preserve edited built-in names and bodies across launches without turning them into custom items. Existing custom-only values remain readable, and unedited built-ins use their bundled defaults. A corrupt or incompatible value remains untouched: an error is shown, bundled built-ins remain usable and all template writes are disabled. A failed write keeps the previous valid value and reports an error. Definition storage never contains draft text or filling sessions. Legacy action-mention visibility/order keys remain stored but are not read or written by the runtime.

The Apple Notes sidebar entry opens its grouped settings form directly. The page focuses on destination, grouping and review controls, retaining grouping and review data-sharing disclosures. It omits the static title explanation, review-opening tutorial, inactive Tags section and redundant destination caption; the title and review instructions live in Quick Guide. Configured services show no AI setup status or shortcut; missing configuration shows one shared hint and an AI Services link for titles, grouping and reviews, while explaining that notes can still be saved separately with a pending title. Existing action-specific settings requests still resolve by stable action ID and may temporarily show another module's detail page; the back button returns to Apple Notes. No generic app list is shown.


Notes offers Allow Access to request macOS Automation access. A successful authorized read keeps a valid saved destination or selects the default folder in the default account, then another account’s default folder or the first exposed folder. Change… opens the optional picker. Setup writes no test content and needs no verification step.

Notes owns authorization, the typed folder picker, grouping and review controls, and persistence. The common settings host uses module declarations. Retired supplement preferences have no runtime effect; original text is protected by application assembly. Notes is the only bundled fallback; if it is unavailable, Set Up Notes appears instead of dispatching another action.

The launcher’s Set Up Notes window offers Allow Access and explains that the default location can be changed in Settings; its folder picker is available only from the settings detail page. Opening either view does not request permission; the user explicitly clicks Allow Access. Background typing and prewarming do not request permission. Successful authorization completes launcher setup automatically and returns to the original text and selection, waiting for another Enter to save. Cancelling also returns to the original draft.

Denied or revoked authorization preserves the saved destination. When authorization or a destination needs repair, the page replaces Change… with Allow Access Again and shows a link to the relevant System Settings privacy controls. The link is hidden when no repair is needed. For Notes, the path is Privacy & Security → Automation → Jotway → Notes. Permission errors retain their meaning instead of appearing as an empty destination list. The app does not reset system permissions or automatically retry an uncertain write.

Unknown and retired action preferences remain stored and inactive; no cleanup migration removes them. Registered modules keep preferences while disabled or unavailable. Notes keeps its folder and tag values. Retired AI enable, supplement prompt and rewrite values stay stored and inactive. Automatic fixed and AI tags remain paused. No Add Extra Ideas switch, supplement editor or legacy rewrite notice is shown. Reopening settings does not delete old values.

## Persistence and secrets

- Template definitions, ordinary preferences, action enablement, retired rewrite and Notes supplement values, tag options, and selected destinations use `UserDefaults`.
- Personal AI provider keys and Jotway AI activation codes are stored in local per-user files with restricted permissions; neither uses the Keychain. Neither is echoed back in the UI.
- Local operation records include stable unsubmitted text and key routing/execution observations. General → Troubleshooting controls capture, retention (90 days by default), manual JSONL/CSV export, storage and completeness status, and clear. Turning capture off also deletes existing records. Inactive legacy rule preferences, application usage, and technical logs are unaffected.
- Operation records never automatically change routing or restore editable drafts after restart.
- Changing a source, model, or prompt invalidates stale connection-test conclusions.

## Connection tests

Connection tests use fixed sample content. They do not send the current draft. A successful connection proves reachability and response shape, not answer quality.

## Runtime logs

General → Troubleshooting → App Logs can open, export, or clear runtime logs. Its scope description remains visible while its controls are collapsed. Export retains raw log lines, including historical Jev metadata, without generating a current routing-rule file. See [Runtime logs](../development/runtime-logs-research.md).

Apple Notes contains a separate Notes Review switch and folder scope. Today/weekly reviews read recent original text on demand from authorized folders, preserving an existing custom scope. There is no background indexing, Search History inspector or embedding service. Bounded review excerpts go to the active text AI service.

Chrome Source defaults to off and is independent of action routing and clipboard history. Enabling it or selecting Allow Chrome Access requests macOS automation permission. Passive checks and copy observations never request permission. The status and retry control remain visible when enabled; denial leaves plain text capture available. Disabling discards unused source candidates but does not remove draft text already shown. The disclosure explains that the current page near a copy may not be the exact source, links may contain private details, and attribution follows existing draft processing settings.

## Clipboard

Actions → Clipboard provides a collection toggle, local-storage disclosure, and a separately confirmed Clear clipboard history button. The history list has no settings navigation; use this application settings page to manage it. Collection defaults on only when no preference is saved, including for users upgrading without a saved choice. Existing enabled and disabled choices stay unchanged across restarts, and turning off default collection persists. Collection remains independent of action enablement and template settings. Enabling starts from the current clipboard version; it does not backfill old copies. Disabling stops collection and OCR without deleting existing history. Clearing removes original text/images, thumbnails and OCR, but leaves the current clipboard unchanged. Storage errors pause collection and remain visible; toggling collection retries the store.

Search and copy remain available with collection off. Images can be searched by cached recognized text. Confirmation copies the original representation; it does not paste into another application.

## Selected Text permission

General → Quick Entry shows Selected Text immediately after shortcut feedback. While the settings window is visible, appearance, window activation and application activation passively check Accessibility for the running process. Check Again starts the same cancellable refresh, bounded to three seconds; it never prompts or reads external text. Only Allow Accessibility requests authorization. Closing, hiding or minimizing settings cancels pending refresh. Capture independently checks current trust on each eligible opening and does not wait for settings retries. Unsupported controls, empty selections and read timeouts do not mean permission was denied. A confirmed grant clears only the permission notice, preserving other errors.

If access remains unavailable, the row keeps its status and permission controls visible and offers a collapsed Troubleshoot Access section with recovery steps and the local running bundle path. The user can compare the authorized entry with the copy they opened. A temporary AppTranslocation path is a clue, not proof of a permission fault. Recovery is manual: save the draft before quitting and reopening the intended installed copy. Jotway does not reset permissions, move or relaunch itself. There is no separate capture toggle or shortcut, and ordinary recording remains available. Notes and Chrome keep their own permissions. See [Selected text](../../openspec/specs/selected-text-capture/spec.md) and [General settings](../../openspec/specs/general-appearance-settings/spec.md).
